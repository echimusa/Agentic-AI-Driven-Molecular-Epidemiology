HOW TO RUN
Exercise 1 (Python 3.12). pip install pandas numpy scipy matplotlib pyreadr
    cd code; cp ../data/measles_hagelloch_1861.RData .; python ex1_hagelloch.py
  Without the data file the script downloads the CRAN package archive (internet needed). Writes figs/.
  Expected: 188 cases, 184 documented infectors; serial interval mean 10.4 d; offspring dispersion k = 0.25; Rt peak 8.1 on day 27; infector ranking top-1 correct 14.7% (timing only) and 40.8% (timing + distance).
Exercise 2 (Python). pip install biopython mappy networkx numpy pandas scipy matplotlib
    cd code; cp ../data/ebola.fasta ../data/ebola_meta.csv .; python ex2_ebola.py
  Writes figs2/ and ranking.csv (identical to data/ebola_candidate_ranking.csv). Expected: 195 genomes kept; 238 variable sites; clock 19.6 SNPs per genome per year (1.04e-03 per site);
  61 early genomes; median effective number of candidate infectors 21.0 (timing only) and 12.5 (timing + genetics).
R companion (R 4.3). install.packages(c("outbreaks", "outbreaker2"))
    cd code; Rscript -e 'source("ex3_outbreaker2.R")'     (about 3 to 5 minutes on one core)
  Expected: top-ranked infector is the documented one for 14.7% of cases; mean posterior probability on the documented infector 10.7%; 3 of 184 above 50%.
Exercise 3 (Python). cd code; cp ../data/measles_hagelloch_1861.RData .; python ex4_descriptive.py     (about a minute)
  Writes the five tables of data/ and figs3/fig11_descriptive.png. Expected: 6 generations of 4, 8, 50, 102, 22 and 2 cases; recorded deaths 12 of 188; the documented infector shares the family in 41.9% of pairs
  against 1.3% of all earlier-later pairs; a 14-day look-back finds 98% of documented infectors with anyone as a candidate (73 per case), 89% with family or class (29), 41% with family alone (1.2).
R companion 2 (TransPhylo 1.4.5; CRAN removed the package on 25 December 2025, so install from the archive):
    install.packages("coda")
    install.packages("https://cran.r-project.org/src/contrib/Archive/TransPhylo/TransPhylo_1.4.5.tar.gz", repos = NULL, type = "source")
    cd code; Rscript ex5_transphylo.R                       (12 runs of 100,000 iterations, about 40 minutes; finished runs are saved as .rds and skipped on a restart)
    TP_ITER=2000 TP_THIN=1 TP_PREFIX=transphylo_short Rscript ex5_transphylo.R       (the same outbreaks with 2,000 iterations, about a minute)
    python ex5_transphylo_analysis.py
  Expected (pooled over six outbreaks per setting): at sampling probability 0.25, 21% of sampled cases have a sampled true infector, top-1 23 of 23, calls above 0.5 18 of 24 true;
  at 0.75, 74%, top-1 52 of 74, 42 of 49. Smallest effective sample size 142 and 106 (100,000 iterations) against 4.3 and 7.1 (2,000). The simulation sets dateStartOutbreak = 2005;
  with the package default start my test ran past five minutes and was stopped.

PROVENANCE AND LIMITS
- The Hagelloch "infector" column is the putative source recorded by the original investigators, not a laboratory-confirmed link. The complications column is recorded as yes for all 188 cases and carries no information.
  Deaths are counted only where a date of death is recorded (12 of 188).
- No true infectors are recorded for the Ebola genomes, so Exercise 2 measures information gain, not accuracy.
- The outbreaker2 run is a single chain; no effective-sample-size check was run. The TransPhylo experiment is a best case: six outbreaks per setting, simulated from the model used to infer them, with the true
  phylogeny supplied and one chain per outbreak. Top-1, precision and recall are pooled over runs (counts summed before the ratios are taken).
- The MRSA and TB-MDR examples cited from Chapter 6 are illustrative scenarios.
- The outbreak and investigation material paraphrases course documents: WHO Module 2.1 (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, non-commercial and share-alike), Larkin and Mavji (year and publisher not stated), the Bhutan Department of Public Health manual
  (2015, scanned and read by OCR), CDC Introduction to Epidemiology (public domain), the USDA APHIS and CFSPH tracing overview (2014) and the Chain of transmission slides (author and year not stated).
- The Part B tutorial calls simulateOutbreak without a start date and runs 2,000 iterations; both can fail as described above.
- Equations in the Word file were verified by converting them back to LaTeX; they were not viewed in Word. Slides were rendered through LibreOffice and not opened in PowerPoint.
