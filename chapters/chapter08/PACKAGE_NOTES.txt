THE DATING STEP IS NOT REPRODUCIBLE
TreeTime (phylo-treetime 0.12.1) gives different dates when the dating step is run again on the same alignment and tree; fixing the random generators of Python and NumPy did not change that. Across ten reruns the date of the
Brazilian common ancestor ranges from 2012.17 to 2012.86 (0.69 years; the chapter run, 2012.44, reports a 90% interval 0.36 years wide), the clock rate from 5.34 to 6.54 x 10^-4, the number of events from 91 to 93 and the number of
introductions into the Americas is 4 (nine reruns) or 3 (one); the main introduction below 487 genomes is the same every time. The files in data/ are the dating of record for the chapter; place reconstruction on them reproduces exactly.
The cause was not pinned down. Report the range over reruns beside any interval.

PROVENANCE AND LIMITS
- Data: public GenBank records for Zika virus downloaded through NCBI Entrez on 8 November 2026; human samples with a country and a collection date (2006-2018), African-lineage sequences excluded so that one lineage sets the clock.
- Dating and place reconstruction use TreeTime on one fixed tree per run; no tree uncertainty is carried, and no BEAST analysis was run. The weak clock signal (root-to-tip R2 = 0.23) was not recomputed by the validation script.
- The estimated introduction into Brazil is earlier than the published estimate (May to December 2013, Faria et al. 2016) in the chapter run and in all ten reruns; it reaches that estimate only when the clock rate is fixed at a faster value.
- Hands-on 5 is a simple simulation: two scenarios, about 140 to 160 sequenced hosts per epidemic, a genealogy with true times, no phylogenetic error and no within-host diversity. Read the direction of the bias and not its size.
- Tajima's D has no significance test (it needs coalescent simulation); the Mantel test treats pairs of countries as independent although they share genomes.
- The tuberculosis audit recomputes the figures printed in the text of Li et al. (2024) from the counts around them: 14 of 22 agree and 8 do not. The supplementary tables were not examined; the mismatches are probably typographical.
- Nextstrain was not run (no Docker or conda runtime in the sandbox); its steps are described from the documentation. The Part B command treetime mugration was run on the chapter's tree.
- The two papers supplied with the course (Chen et al. 2024; Li et al. 2024) are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 and are paraphrased, not adapted. The Garrido-Benavent slides carry no licence statement and are paraphrased.
- Equations in the Word file were verified by converting them back to LaTeX; they were not viewed in Word. Slides were rendered through LibreOffice and not opened in PowerPoint.
